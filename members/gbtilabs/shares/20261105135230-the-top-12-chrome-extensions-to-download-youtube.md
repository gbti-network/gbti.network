Vidito compares 12 ways to download YouTube videos through Chrome, but many are not conventional Web Store extensions at all. The list spans sideloaded add-ons, Tampermonkey scripts, browser helpers, and desktop download managers such as JDownloader and IDM.[^1]

There is a reason for the unusual installation methods. Chrome Web Store guidance specifically lists facilitating YouTube video downloads as a common reason an extension may be removed or rejected under its prohibited-content rules.[^2]

Vidito compares the options by reliability, formats, resolution, price, and installation complexity, then separates quick browser-based approaches from tools better suited to playlists and large download queues. The guide also covers safety, copyright considerations, and YouTube Premium as the official offline-viewing option.[^3]
