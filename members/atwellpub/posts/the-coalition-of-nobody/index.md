A literary idea: an international coalition whose sole condition for membership is that one's legal first name be Nobody.

The coalition seeks to locate and unite every Nobody in the world. Anybody may join, provided they legally become Nobody.

An interesting paradox emerges. As more people become Nobody, Nobody becomes increasingly influential. An otherwise insignificant name becomes the foundation of an exclusive yet universally accessible collective identity.

Membership is fiercely guarded. Only Nobody knows who belongs to the coalition, though one might reasonably suspect Nobody's affiliation by his first name alone.

Perhaps one day Nobody will change the world.

**Notable Nobodies in literary history**

**Odysseus (c. 8th century BC):** Identifies himself as Nobody in Homer's *The Odyssey*, deceiving the Cyclops Polyphemus.

**Nemo (1510):** Ulrich von Hutten's literary personification of Nobody. Nemo is Latin for "no one."

**Captain Nemo (1870):** Jules Verne's mysterious captain in *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas*, who adopts Nobody as his identity.

**Nobody Owens (2008):** The protagonist of Neil Gaiman's *The Graveyard Book*, a boy raised by the inhabitants of a cemetery.

**Disclaimer:** The Coalition of Nobody is a fictional organization that occasionally visits the mind of a man, perhaps as an invitation to change his name, or perhaps to establish a new branch. We suspect only Nobody truly knows. As the author's first name is not Nobody, no invitation is extended by this publication.[^cover]
